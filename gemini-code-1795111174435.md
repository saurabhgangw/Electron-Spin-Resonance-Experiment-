## 1. Critical Examination of *Sparks of Weight-Space Programming* (Takehi et al., 2026)

### Strongest Evidence
- **Analytical Circuit Recovery Without Forward Passes (Experiment 3):** Given only the four middle transformer blocks (7.16M weights) of character-level models trained on Python, Romaji, and English—with input/output embeddings stripped and forward passes strictly prohibited—`GPT-6 Astra` identified all three training languages and recovered the exact induction/copying heads (Python: `2.1, 3.1, 3.5`; Romaji: `3.3`; English: `3.1`) via weight-matrix composition, independently rediscovering the framework of Elhage et al. (2021).
- **Surgical Failure-Mode Ablation at Sub-Billion Scale (Experiment 8):** Using a Python kernel and 50 forward-pass batches (no gradients), Astra traced `Qwen3.5-0.8B`’s severe reasoning loops (93.0% hitting the 32,768-token limit) to a single direction in the MLPs of layers 15 and 19. Subtracting that direction from 64 output columns per layer (131,072 weights, or 0.016% of parameters) cut token-limit hits to 33.1%, lifted MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% and GSM8K from 13.8% to 67.1%, and transferred out-of-domain to IFEval (21.0% → 62.3%).
- **Zero-Shot Structural Reading and Synthesis on Compact MLPs (Experiments 1, 2, 4, 5):** On small MLPs ($\le 171\text{k}$ weights), Astra inferred game mechanics from raw weights (e.g., paddle and brick rows in Breakout), reconstructed geometric shapes from DDPM weights as *outlines* rather than filled images (confirming static geometric reading rather than implicit forward passes), repaired a stalled Kinetix policy in one shot by editing 3.5% of weights (0% → 100% success), and synthesized MinAtar policies from zero weights that beat 100M-step PPO.

### Most Important Limitations & Unjustified Conclusions
- **Static Weight Legibility Fails on Post-Trained LLMs (Experiment 7):** When asked to identify which of two `Gemma 3 270M` checkpoints (with frozen embeddings) was fine-tuned for sycophancy, Astra **failed in all four attempts** from raw weights alone. It succeeded (3/3 pairs) only when granted an **attention-pattern tool** running forward passes on up to 16 self-written probes (isolating head `10.2`, which shifted 85.7% attention to the user’s suggested answer versus 0.75% in the honest model). Static weight reading breaks down once behaviors are distributed across post-trained transformer layers.
- **Direct Weight Reasoning vs. Closed-Loop Feedback Optimization:** Scaling beyond tiny MLPs to 1.48M-parameter Go (Experiment 6) and `Qwen3.5-0.8B` (Experiment 8) relied heavily on a Python harness plus **50 forward-pass evaluation batches** (up to $50 \times 512 = 25,600$ problem rollouts in Experiment 8). Crucially, at 10 and 25 forward passes in Experiment 8, MATH-500 barely improved (7.9% → 12.3% → 14.1%), jumping to 48.1% only at 50 forward passes. Real-LLM weight edits therefore depend on iterative empirical feedback rather than one-shot static weight comprehension.
- **Obstacle Removal vs. Capability Creation (Experiment 8 Follow-Up):** On `Gemma 3 270M`—which rarely loops (8.5% token-limit hits versus 93.0% for Qwen)—Astra failed to improve MATH-500 (6.8% base versus 7.0% at 50 forward passes). As Takehi et al. acknowledge: *"Editing weights could remove an obstacle that hid an ability, but it could not add an ability the model lacked."*
- **What Is and Isn't Justified:** The evidence supports using LLM-guided weight and activation inspection as a targeted debugging workflow to ablate localized, low-rank failure modes when a forward-pass oracle is available. It does **not** justify treating production LLM weights as directly readable source code or expecting weight edits to instill new linguistic, cultural, or reasoning capabilities without training data.

## 2. Hypothesis

- **Research Question:** On a compact open-weight causal LM (`Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B`), can a targeted weight-space edit eliminate English language leakage on uninstructed Swedish prompts without degrading general factual/arithmetic accuracy—and do observed shifts come from single-pass direct weight attribution or closed-loop feedback search (and reflect removing a surface routing bias rather than adding new Swedish capability)?
- **Why It Matters for SE.LLMA:** Funded by the Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation within WASP (Linköping, Umeå, and Uppsala Universities with NAISS), SE.LLMA adapts existing open-weight language models iteratively from smaller to larger scales using licensed Swedish public-sector, editorial, and literary corpora under strict copyright and transparency frameworks. Suppressing English fallback without catastrophic forgetting is essential when adapting multilingual base models to Swedish. Testing whether weight edits outperform simple prompting or activation steering clarifies whether SE.LLMA should rely on curated Swedish training data versus surgical weight-space interventions.
- **Supporting vs. Challenging Evidence:**
  - *Supporting:* Targeted weight edits outperform both the unchanged baseline and norm-matched random perturbations on held-out `TEST` Swedish prompts while preserving `REGRESSION` accuracy.
  - *Challenging:* Gains fail to generalize from `DEV` to `TEST`, match random noise of identical $L_2$ norm, degrade unrelated general capability, or remain weaker than simple prompting or activation steering (indicating that Swedish competence is already latent in the weights).

## 3. Experimental Setup & Controls

- **Model, Compute Adaptation & Reproducibility:** To fit the 4-hour CPU-only budget cleanly while mirroring SE.LLMA’s small-to-large open-model workflow, we evaluate `Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B` (Apache-2.0 open weights, `seed=42`, deterministic greedy decoding; exact resolved Hugging Face commit hash logged in `results/metrics.json`).
- **Strict Train/Eval Separation:**
  - `DEV_PAIRS` (6 Swedish vs. English minimal pairs): Used exclusively for gradient-weight attribution, evolutionary search feedback, and contrastive steering-vector extraction.
  - `TEST_PAIRS` (10 disjoint Swedish vs. English pairs): Strictly held-out target evaluation measuring Swedish win rate and mean sequence log-probability margin $\Delta \log P = \log P(\text{sv} \mid x) - \log P(\text{en} \mid x)$.
  - `REGRESSION_PAIRS` (8 unrelated English arithmetic and factual prompts): Measures collateral capability degradation.
- **Six Controlled Conditions (Matched Frobenius Norm $\|\Delta W\|_F$ Across All Weight Edits):**
  1. **Unchanged Baseline:** Unmodified pre-trained weights.
  2. **Prompting Control:** Unmodified weights with a 1-line Swedish system instruction (`"Instruktion: Svara alltid på naturlig och korrekt svenska."`).
  3. **Contrastive Activation Steering:** Inference-time residual-stream addition at 2/3 network depth ($\alpha = 2.0$), unchanged weights.
  4. **Direct Weight Attribution:** Single-pass gradient-weight product row selection and sign update on the final block’s `mlp.down_proj.weight` (**zero** closed-loop feedback iterations).
  5. **Feedback Evolutionary Edit:** $(1+\lambda)$ zero-order search over `mlp.down_proj.weight` guided by repeated `DEV_PAIRS` margin evaluations, projected onto the exact same $L_2$ norm sphere.
  6. **Random Norm-Matched Control:** Untargeted Gaussian perturbation scaled to the identical $L_2$ norm.

## 5. Recommendation for SE.LLMA

### Should SE.LLMA Investigate Weight-Space Programming Further?
- **No as a substitute for Swedish training; Yes selectively as an auditable post-training diagnostic.** Consistent with Takehi et al.’s negative result on `Gemma 3 270M`, weight-space editing cannot inject Swedish cultural grounding, historical context, legal precision, or specialized domain vocabulary—those require SE.LLMA’s licensed Swedish public-sector, journalistic, and literary corpora via continual pre-training, SFT, and DPO. However, because SE.LLMA builds iteratively on existing open-weight models, LLM-assisted activation and weight inspection (Experiment 8’s paradigm) is useful as a surgical debugging tool to locate and ablate inherited base-model pathologies (such as repetition loops or language-switching glitches) without full retraining.
- **Operational, Governance & AI Infrastructure Trade-Offs (NAISS):** Weight edits introduce zero serving-time latency compared to prompt prefixes (prefill token tax) or runtime activation steering (custom hooks inside vLLM/TensorRT-LLM), and sparse mechanistic patches ($<0.1\%$ of weights) maintain clear auditability aligned with SE.LLMA’s transparency principles. Conversely, closed-loop feedback weight search scales poorly with parameter count ($O(\text{steps} \times \lambda)$ forward passes per probe) and complicates checkpoint lineage compared to modular **LoRA adapters**, which integrate natively with distributed GPU training on NAISS, multi-tenant inference servers, and automated CI regression pipelines.

### Most Informative Next Experiment
- **Small-to-Large Iterative Pareto Benchmark:** Aligned with SE.LLMA’s iterative scaling roadmap, compare (1) **rank-8 LoRA SFT/DPO on licensed Swedish text**, (2) **multi-layer contrastive activation steering**, and (3) **sparse MLP direction ablation** (Experiment 8’s protocol) across both a small (0.5B–1.5B) and medium (7B–8B) open checkpoint. Evaluate on **ScandEval / SuperLim** (Swedish linguistic and cultural quality) alongside general reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU) and NAISS GPU-hour cost to determine whether surgical weight ablation complements domain fine-tuning during scaling.
